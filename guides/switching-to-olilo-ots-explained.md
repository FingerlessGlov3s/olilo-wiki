# Switching to Olilo (OTS) Explained

If you're moving from another Openreach or CityFibre-based provider, switching uses **One Touch Switching (OTS)**, an Ofcom-mandated process (mandatory industry-wide since April 2024) that moves your line without a "double install" or overlap gap.

## How it works

1. **You place your order.** Pick a plan, tell us you're switching from another provider.
2. **Your old provider is notified automatically.** You don't need to give them notice yourself, or call to cancel.
3. **Within 24 hours you'll get two letters**, one from your old provider (a "Switching Information Notice" showing any early termination charges you might owe them), and one from Olilo (a "Contract Information Notice" confirming your new plan, price, and go-live date).
4. **The switch happens on a single date.** There's only ever seconds of overlap between providers, not days.
5. **You're live**, with your login credentials (PPPoE details, or your L2TP details if applicable) sent by Olilo.

Typical switches complete within **10 working days** of placing your order.

## Cooling-off period

You have **14 days from your order date** (not your go-live date) to cancel penalty-free. If installation work has already started before you cancel, you may be liable for the cost of that work, but you'll never be charged an Early Termination Charge during the cooling-off window.

## What OTS doesn't cover

OTS only moves **broadband**. If you have landline or VoIP with your old provider, that isn't ported automatically, sort this separately if you need to keep a number.

## Fees that can apply

- **£17 migration charge**, only on 30-day rolling contracts, only for Openreach/CityFibre network transfers, charged at cost. Not charged on 12 or 24-month contracts.
- **£60 cessation fee**, only applies if you're on a rolling plan from the outset on the Openreach, Trooli, or MS3 networks, and you cancel within your first 12 months. Charged at cost. Doesn't apply to CityFibre or Freedom Fibre. See T&Cs §12.1 for the full wording.

Neither fee is an Early Termination Charge, see [Contract Types and Cancelling Explained](./contract-types-and-cancelling-explained) for how ETCs actually work.
